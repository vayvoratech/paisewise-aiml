STOCKS
Development RAG Knowledge Source

A stock, also called an equity share, generally represents an ownership interest in a company. Some shares may provide voting rights and some companies may distribute dividends.

Stock prices can change because of company performance, earnings expectations, economic conditions, investor sentiment, news, interest rates, industry developments, and supply and demand.

Market capitalization is commonly calculated as market price per share multiplied by shares outstanding. It is often used to describe the size of a publicly traded company.

A dividend is a distribution made by a company to eligible shareholders. Companies are not necessarily required to pay dividends, and payments can change or stop.

If a security is sold for more than its purchase price, the difference may represent a capital gain before applicable costs and taxes. Selling below the purchase price may result in a capital loss.

Volatility describes the degree to which an asset's price fluctuates. A stock can be affected by company-specific risks such as competition, management decisions, regulation, debt, and product demand, as well as broad market risk.

Explaining stocks is educational. Identifying a specific stock as suitable for a particular person would be personalized investment advice and is outside the scope of this knowledge base.
