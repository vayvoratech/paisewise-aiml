MARKET BASICS
Development RAG Knowledge Source

Financial markets are systems in which financial assets can be issued, bought, and sold.

The primary market is where securities are issued to investors. An initial public offering (IPO) is one example. The secondary market is where existing securities are traded among market participants.

Market prices are influenced by buying and selling interest. Changes in expectations can alter demand and supply.

A market index is a measure designed to represent the performance of a selected group of securities or a defined market segment.

A bull market commonly describes a period of broadly rising prices, while a bear market commonly describes a period of broadly declining prices.

Interest-rate changes can affect borrowing costs, bond valuations, company financing conditions, and investor expectations. Inflation, employment, economic growth, and other indicators can influence markets.

Markets can experience periods of rapid price movement. Volatility can increase around economic events, corporate announcements, geopolitical developments, or changes in expectations.

New information can cause investors to revise expectations, which can affect prices.

Market explanations describe general mechanisms. They are not predictions of future market movements or recommendations to trade.
