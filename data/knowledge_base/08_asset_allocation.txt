ASSET ALLOCATION
Development RAG Knowledge Source

Asset allocation is the distribution of investments across categories such as equities, bonds, cash, and other assets.

Asset allocation structures portfolio exposure across investments with different characteristics. A person's investment time horizon can influence how different assets may be considered, but the appropriate allocation is an individual decision.

Risk tolerance describes how much uncertainty or loss an investor is willing or able to accept. A general-purpose assistant should not assume an individual's risk tolerance.

Asset allocation and diversification are related but different. Asset allocation distributes exposure among asset classes, while diversification can also occur within an asset class.

A portfolio's allocation can change as asset prices move. Rebalancing is the process of bringing the portfolio back toward a chosen allocation according to a defined approach.

Strategic allocation generally establishes longer-term target exposures. Tactical allocation refers to shorter-term changes intended to respond to market views or conditions.

A financial-learning assistant can explain asset-allocation concepts but should not prescribe an individual's target percentages or tell an individual exactly how to allocate money.
