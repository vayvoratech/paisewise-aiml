MUTUAL FUNDS
Development RAG Knowledge Source

A mutual fund pools money from multiple investors and invests according to a defined objective and strategy. A fund may invest in stocks, bonds, money-market instruments, or other securities.

A fund's NAV represents the value of its assets minus liabilities under its applicable valuation methodology. Fund transactions are processed according to the fund's dealing and valuation rules.

Mutual funds can provide diversification because a single fund may hold many securities. Diversification does not eliminate market, credit, interest-rate, liquidity, currency, or strategy-specific risks.

An actively managed fund generally uses a manager or team to select investments. A passive fund generally seeks to track an index or defined benchmark.

Funds can have management fees, operating expenses, transaction-related costs, distribution expenses, or other charges. Investors should review official fund documents for objectives, strategy, holdings, risks, fees, performance information, and terms.

A general explanation of mutual funds should not be interpreted as a recommendation to purchase or sell a particular fund.
