Inflation refers to a general increase in prices over time. When prices increase, the purchasing power of money decreases. For example, the amount required to purchase a particular product in the future may be higher than the amount required today. Inflation is important when planning long-term financial goals because simply saving the same nominal amount may not be sufficient to meet future expenses. Investment returns are therefore often considered in relation to inflation. Nominal return represents the stated investment return, while real return considers the effect of inflation. Understanding inflation helps individuals estimate future expenses and evaluate whether their financial plans account for changing purchasing power.
