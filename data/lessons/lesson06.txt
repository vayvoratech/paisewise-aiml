Compounding occurs when returns earned on money are added to the original amount and subsequently generate additional returns. Over longer periods, this can have a significant effect on accumulated wealth. For example, if money earns returns and those returns remain invested, future returns can be generated on both the original amount and previous returns. The effect depends on the return rate, investment period and frequency of compounding. Compounding also applies to borrowing, where unpaid interest can increase the amount owed depending on the loan terms. Understanding compounding helps explain why time can be an important factor in long-term financial planning.
