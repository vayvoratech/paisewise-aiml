# PaiseWise Cultural Terminology & Native Analogy List

## Purpose

This document identifies financial education terms that may need
native-language explanations or culturally familiar analogies.

Supported languages:

- English (en)
- Hindi (hi)
- Tamil (ta)
- Telugu (te)
- Kannada (kn)
- Malayalam (ml)

The purpose is to make financial education easier to understand
without changing the actual financial meaning.

---

# 1. Mutual Fund

## Term
Mutual Fund

## Why localization is useful
Some users may understand mutual funds more easily when explained
as money collected from many people and managed together.

## Suggested analogy

### Hindi
"कई लोगों द्वारा मिलकर पैसा जमा करके एक जगह निवेश करना।"

### Tamil
"பலர் சேர்ந்து பணத்தை ஒன்றாக முதலீடு செய்வது."

### Telugu
"చాలా మంది కలిసి డబ్బును ఒకచోట పెట్టుబడి పెట్టడం."

### Kannada
"ಅನೇಕ ಜನರು ಹಣವನ್ನು ಒಟ್ಟಾಗಿ ಸೇರಿಸಿ ಹೂಡಿಕೆ ಮಾಡುವುದು."

### Malayalam
"പലരും ചേർന്ന് പണം ഒരുമിച്ച് നിക്ഷേപിക്കുന്നത്."

# 2. SIP

## Term
SIP - Systematic Investment Plan

## Why localization is useful
Explain SIP using the idea of regularly setting aside a fixed
amount instead of investing a large amount at once.

## Suggested analogy

### Hindi
"हर महीने थोड़ी-थोड़ी बचत करके निवेश करना।"

### Tamil
"ஒவ்வொரு மாதமும் ஒரு குறிப்பிட்ட தொகையை தொடர்ந்து முதலீடு செய்வது."

### Telugu
"ప్రతి నెల కొంత మొత్తాన్ని క్రమం తప్పకుండా పెట్టుబడి పెట్టడం."

### Kannada
"ಪ್ರತಿ ತಿಂಗಳು ನಿಗದಿತ ಮೊತ್ತವನ್ನು ನಿಯಮಿತವಾಗಿ ಹೂಡಿಕೆ ಮಾಡುವುದು."

### Malayalam
"ഓരോ മാസവും ഒരു നിശ്ചിത തുക സ്ഥിരമായി നിക്ഷേപിക്കുന്നത്."

# 3. Diversification

## Term
Diversification

## Why localization is useful
Users can understand diversification through the idea of not putting
all resources into one place.

## Suggested analogy

### Hindi
"सारा पैसा एक ही जगह रखने के बजाय अलग-अलग जगह निवेश करना।"

### Tamil
"முழு பணத்தையும் ஒரே இடத்தில் வைக்காமல் பல இடங்களில் முதலீடு செய்வது."

### Telugu
"మొత్తం డబ్బును ఒకే చోట పెట్టకుండా వివిధ పెట్టుబడుల్లో పెట్టడం."

### Kannada
"ಎಲ್ಲಾ ಹಣವನ್ನು ಒಂದೇ ಕಡೆ ಇಡದೆ ವಿವಿಧ ಹೂಡಿಕೆಗಳಲ್ಲಿ ಹಂಚುವುದು."

### Malayalam
"മുഴുവൻ പണവും ഒരിടത്ത് നിക്ഷേപിക്കാതെ വിവിധ നിക്ഷേപങ്ങളായി വിഭജിക്കുന്നത്."

# 4. Risk

## Term
Investment Risk

## Why localization is useful
Risk should be explained as the possibility that the investment value
may change or that the expected return may not happen.

## Important rule

Do not describe investment risk as guaranteed loss.

Use:
"possibility of loss or lower-than-expected return."

---

# 5. Return

## Term
Investment Return

## Why localization is useful
Users may understand return better when explained as the gain or loss
from an investment over a period.

## Important rule

Avoid promising future returns.

Use educational examples only.

---

# 6. Inflation

## Term
Inflation

## Why localization is useful
Inflation is easier to understand through everyday prices.

## Suggested analogy

Use familiar examples such as:

- food prices
- household groceries
- transportation costs
- school/education expenses

Example:

"If the same amount of money buys fewer groceries over time,
that illustrates the effect of inflation."

---

# 7. Emergency Fund

## Term
Emergency Fund

## Why localization is useful
Use familiar unexpected expenses.

## Suggested examples

- medical expenses
- temporary loss of income
- urgent household repairs
- unexpected education expenses

Do not assume a specific family structure.

---

# 8. Compounding

## Term
Compound Growth / Compounding

## Why localization is useful
Users may understand compounding better when shown how returns
can themselves generate additional returns over time.

## Important rule

Use simple numerical examples.

Do not promise that actual investments will produce the same result.

---

# 9. Market Volatility

## Term
Market Volatility

## Why localization is useful
Explain volatility as frequent upward and downward movement in
investment prices.

## Suggested analogy

"Like a road with frequent ups and downs, the market can move
up and down over short periods."

The analogy should not imply that market movements are predictable.

---

# 10. Large Cap / Mid Cap / Small Cap

## Terms

- Large Cap
- Mid Cap
- Small Cap

## Localization requirement

Keep the official financial terms in English when appropriate,
but explain them in the user's native language.

Do not replace official fund/category terminology with an
informal local term if doing so could create confusion.

---

# 11. NAV

## Term
NAV - Net Asset Value

## Localization requirement

Keep "NAV" unchanged.

Explain it in the user's language as the per-unit value of a
mutual fund.

---

# 12. Expense Ratio

## Term
Expense Ratio

## Localization requirement

Explain it as the percentage charged from the fund for managing
the investment.

Keep the official term available because users may encounter it
in financial documents.

---

# 13. Equity

## Term
Equity

## Localization requirement

Explain equity as ownership represented through shares of a company.

Avoid translating the term in a way that changes its financial meaning.

---

# 14. Debt

## Term
Debt Investment

## Localization requirement

Explain debt investments using the idea of lending money to an
issuer in exchange for interest or other contractual returns.

Avoid using everyday "debt" meanings that could confuse users.

---

# 15. Portfolio

## Term
Portfolio

## Localization requirement

Use the user's language to explain portfolio as the collection of
investments owned by an investor.

Suggested analogy:

"Like a collection containing different types of investments."

# Language-Specific Content Guidelines

## Hindi

- Use simple conversational Hindi.
- Keep commonly recognized financial terms such as SIP, NAV and
  mutual fund when useful.
- Avoid overly formal Hindi terminology if it reduces comprehension.

## Tamil

- Use natural conversational Tamil.
- Preserve commonly recognized financial abbreviations.
- Avoid literal translations that sound unnatural.

## Telugu

- Use simple conversational Telugu.
- Preserve commonly recognized financial terms such as SIP, NAV and
  mutual fund where appropriate.
- Prefer examples familiar to everyday household financial planning.

## Kannada

- Use natural conversational Kannada.
- Preserve standard financial abbreviations.
- Avoid word-for-word translations when the result sounds unnatural.

## Malayalam

- Use simple conversational Malayalam.
- Preserve standard financial abbreviations.
- Prefer clear explanations over highly formal financial vocabulary.

---

# Content Team Rules

1. Preserve the original financial meaning.
2. Do not introduce investment recommendations.
3. Do not promise returns.
4. Do not imply guaranteed profits.
5. Do not use culturally specific examples that could exclude users.
6. Use local examples only to improve understanding.
7. Keep numerical examples mathematically unchanged.
8. Keep stock symbols and financial abbreviations unchanged where
   appropriate.
9. Review translations with native-language speakers.
10. Update this document when new financial concepts are added.
